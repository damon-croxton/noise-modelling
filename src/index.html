A shift is one activity in one time period, with its own plant and dates. Buildings, NCAs and the method are shared. Click a shift to show it on the map.

Click a row to select and edit it, or drag it on the map. Levels are the effective sound power after % time on, penalty and mitigation.

Results update by themselves after you change the plant, buildings or method (if Update automatically is ticked). Run all shifts is only needed to compare shifts or check respite – Compare & respite does it for you.

The report is an HTML file: open it in Edge and print to PDF. The report and the CSV list properties and may contain addresses – keep them on approved storage.

You are looking at a synthetic example (no real location). Follow steps 1–3 to start your own project at your site – the example is cleared when you go to the site.
  1. Copy the site location. In Google Maps, right-click the project location, then click the coordinates at the top of the menu (for example -33.88901, 151.23702) – that copies them. Paste them here:
  2. Click Go to site. The map moves there and shows the OSM street map for your bearings.
  3. Click Fetch OSM buildings. Downloads the building footprints in view from OpenStreetMap (zoom so the view is about 1 km across or less).
    If the fetch fails

    Your network may block it. Click Copy OSM download link, paste the link into a new Edge tab, save the file it downloads (rename it map.osm), then click Import buildings and pick that file.

Optional – improves accuracy

  1. Terrain: download a 1 m DEM for the site from the ELVIS portal and load it in Terrain (DEM) below. Improves accuracy with the ground surface profile (cuttings, embankments, hills).
  2. Building heights: download LiDAR point cloud tiles (.laz) for the site from ELVIS and load them in Building heights from LiDAR. Improves accuracy of building heights – estimated heights are shown with dashed outlines.

In ELVIS, find the site, pick a LiDAR point-cloud product covering it and download the .laz tile(s). Some datasets download straight away; others email a link. Then load the file here.

No LiDAR loaded. Class 6 (building) points give roof levels; class 2 (ground) builds the terrain if no DEM is loaded. The file is read locally – nothing is uploaded.

In ELVIS, find the site and download a 1 m DEM (GeoTIFF or ASCII grid) covering the works and the nearby buildings. Then load it here.

An NCA sets the background level (RBL) and so the NML for the buildings inside it. Draw them, import them, or use the default below for buildings outside every NCA.

Buildings with no land use in OSM are assumed residential. Change many at once here, or one at a time by selecting a building.

Each building is assessed on its exposed façades at 1.5 m above each floor; the worst point per floor is reported.

Import measured levels or another tool's receiver results (CSV with a receiver ID, or easting/northing or lat/lon with height, floor or RL, and an LAeq column) to see how this tool compares.

Screening and planning aid only. It does not replace an assessment by a qualified acoustic consultant and is not a validated compliance model. Plant levels and assessment defaults come from Transport for NSW documents – verify them against the current client strategy and replace them with project data where available. The DEM and LiDAR are not saved in the project file – reload them after opening it.

Buildings: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). LAZ decoding: JavaScript port of LASzip by rapidlasso GmbH (Apache License 2.0). Plant levels, air and ground values: Transport for NSW, CNVG-PTI EMF-NV-GD-0060 v4.6 and the Construction Noise Estimator EMF-NV-TT-0068, used with acknowledgement. Methods: ISO 9613-1, ISO 9613-2, CONCAWE. Details: THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md in the source repository.


  






  

    
    
Quick guide

    
    
      
  1. Site. Site and buildings: paste the site coordinates from Google Maps (right-click → click the coordinates), click Go to site, then Fetch OSM buildings. Optionally load a 1 m DEM and LiDAR from ELVIS. Draw or import the noise catchment areas.
    2. 
      
  2. Shift. In Shifts, name the activity, pick the time period and enter the dates. Click + Plant (or press S) and click the map where each item will work. Pick the equipment in Selected.
    3. 
      
  3. Results. The first time, click Run this shift (or the status button at the top). After that the results update automatically when you change something. The map colours each building by its worst impact category; hover for the calculation.
    4. 
      
  4. More shifts. + New shift or Duplicate, then change the plant, period or dates. Click a shift chip to switch. Compare & respite runs any shift that is out of date and checks the respite rules – you don't need to click Run all shifts first.
    5. 
      
  5. Outputs. Create report (Results / Report step) makes the printable report for the shift shown; Export CSV gives the property list for the community team. Both may contain addresses.

What the buttons do

    

      
Status button (top)Shows whether the current shift's results match the inputs: Up to date Out of date Not run. Click it to run now.

      
Run this shiftAssesses every building and floor for the shift on the map.

      
Run all shiftsAssesses every shift in turn. Only needed for comparisons, respite or a report that covers all shifts.

      
Compare & respiteTable of every shift and every affected property, with the CNVG-PTI respite-period check. Runs out-of-date shifts first.

      
File / Clear / ViewSave and open projects; remove things or tidy the screen; change layout, colours and map layers.

      
Focus (F)Hides the panels to show only the map. Press F or Esc to bring them back.
